Temporal Behaviour

Monthly escalation rates vary over the 2025–2026 period rather than remaining perfectly constant. Because the task has a meaningful time dimension, random cross-validation could provide an overly optimistic estimate of generalization.

This observation motivated a chronological validation strategy: models are trained on earlier alerts and evaluated on later periods.

Feature Engineering

Millions of transaction rows are aggregated into one behavioural feature vector for each AML alert.

V

Volume

Total transaction counts, incoming/outgoing counts, transaction-type counts, and recent transaction counts.

A

Amount

Mean, median, minimum, maximum, and standard deviation of transaction amount indices.

R

Recency

Activity within 1-day, 7-day, 30-day, and 90-day windows before each alert.

T

Timing

Transaction-hour behaviour plus night-time and weekend activity.

C

Conditional Behaviour

Amount statistics for card, bank-transfer, cash, and outgoing transactions.

Temporal Leakage Control

AML prediction must use information available at the time of the alert. Transactions occurring after the alert could reveal future information and artificially inflate validation performance.

Every feature is calculated only from transactions satisfying tranzaksiya_vaqti <= signal_sanasi. After filtering, the pipeline verifies that no post-alert transactions remain.

Final Model

The final prediction architecture is an equal-weight ensemble of five regularized LightGBM models trained on the same 53-feature alert representation with different random seeds.

The number of boosting iterations for each seed is selected from the median best iteration across the chronological validation folds. The five final models are then trained on all 14,000 training alerts and their predicted probabilities are averaged equally.
07

Key Findings

01

Activity Matters

Escalated alerts have moderately higher transaction activity, but substantial overlap remains between classes.

02

Simple Shares Are Not Enough

Direction and transaction-type proportions are similar across classes, motivating richer behavioural statistics.

03

Time Matters

Escalation behaviour varies over time, supporting chronological rather than random validation.

04

Leakage Control Matters

Post-alert transactions must be excluded so that features reflect information available when an alert occurs.

The final system combines leakage-safe transaction aggregation, explicit ID alignment, chronological validation, and a regularized gradient-boosting ensemble to prioritize AML alerts.
